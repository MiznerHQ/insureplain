Missed Open Enrollment?
Here's What Actually Happens Next

ENROLLMENT

Written by the InsurePlain Editorial Team · Reviewed for accuracy · Last updated August 2026

⚠️ Educational only. InsurePlain does not provide insurance, legal, or financial advice. Always consult a qualified insurance professional about your specific situation.
What's actually happening

If the annual open enrollment window closed and you didn't sign up, you're not automatically locked out of health coverage for the rest of the year — but you also can't just walk into the marketplace tomorrow and enroll like nothing happened. Outside of open enrollment, marketplace plans are only available to people who qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, triggered by specific life changes.

That distinction matters more than most people realize. Missing the deadline itself isn't the disqualifying event — it's whether something else happened in your life recently that opens a new window. Job loss, marriage, a new baby, moving to a new coverage area, and a handful of other changes all count. Simply forgetting the date, on its own, typically does not.

The good news: most people who think they've missed their only shot still have a real path back to coverage. The key is figuring out which path applies to your situation, and moving quickly once you know — because these windows are short.

It also helps to separate two things people tend to lump together: the annual open enrollment window itself, and a Special Enrollment Period triggered by a life event. They're not the same mechanism, they don't run on the same clock, and confusing them is often what makes people give up on coverage they actually still qualify for.

Quick answers

What people ask most

Can I still get health insurance after open enrollment ends?
Yes, if you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period or a program that enrolls year-round. Medicaid and CHIP don't follow the annual marketplace calendar at all, and a wide range of life events reopen marketplace enrollment for 60 days.
Will I owe a penalty for not having health insurance?
There's no federal tax penalty for being uninsured as of now. A few states run their own individual mandates with state-level penalties, so it depends on where you live — check your state's rules directly.
How long until the next open enrollment period?
Marketplace open enrollment typically runs once a year, in the fall. If you don't qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, you may be looking at a genuine coverage gap until that window reopens — which is exactly why it's worth checking every possible SEP trigger first.
Your options right now

Life events that reopen enrollment

A Special Enrollment Period is triggered by a "qualifying life event" — a defined list of changes the marketplace recognizes as a legitimate reason you need coverage outside the normal window. These aren't loosely interpreted; each one has to be documented, and the window is calculated from the date the event actually happened, not from when you realized it mattered. If any of these happened to you in roughly the last 60 days, you likely qualify:

  • Job loss or reduced hours — losing employer coverage opens a 60-day window, even if the job ended after open enrollment closed.
  • Marriage or divorce — a change in marital status is a standard qualifying event.
  • Birth, adoption, or foster placement of a child — new dependents trigger their own enrollment window.
  • Turning 26 and aging off a parent's plan — this is treated the same as losing other job-based coverage.
  • Moving to a new ZIP code or county — especially if it changes which plans are available to you.
  • A change in household income that affects subsidy eligibility — this can open a window even without a coverage loss.
  • Loss of other minimum essential coverage — this includes losing coverage through a parent, spouse, or a previous marketplace plan that was cancelled or non-renewed.
Comparing your realistic paths

Special enrollment vs. waiting it out

Once you know whether a qualifying life event applies, your path forward looks very different depending on the answer.

If you have a qualifying event

You get a 60-day window to enroll in a marketplace plan, starting from the date of the event — not from when open enrollment closed. Coverage can often start the first of the following month.

If you don't

Your marketplace options are limited until the next annual open enrollment period. Medicaid or CHIP may still be available year-round if your income qualifies, and a short-term or bridge plan may be worth considering.

⚠️ THE 60-DAY CLOCK IS UNFORGIVING

Special Enrollment Periods almost always run exactly 60 days from the qualifying event — not 60 days from when you find out about it or get around to applying. Waiting even a few weeks to confirm eligibility can cost you the entire window. If in doubt, apply first and sort out documentation after.

What to check, in order

Your next 5 steps

  1. STEP 1. List anything that changed in your life or job in the last 60 days — even things that don't feel insurance-related, like a move or a new part-time job.
  2. STEP 2. Check whether that change matches a recognized qualifying life event, using the list above.
  3. STEP 3. If it matches, apply through the marketplace immediately — don't wait for paperwork to be perfect first.
  4. STEP 4. If it doesn't match, check your household income against Medicaid and CHIP eligibility, since those enroll year-round regardless of open enrollment.
  5. STEP 5. If neither applies, weigh a short-term plan or COBRA continuation as a bridge until the next open enrollment period.
What to do right now
Don't assume you're out of options just because a date passed. Start by checking the qualifying life event list against your last 60 days, and apply the moment you find a match — the clock runs from the event, not from today. If nothing matches, check Medicaid and CHIP eligibility before ruling out coverage entirely.
Frequently asked questions
Does losing a job right after open enrollment closed count as a qualifying event?
Yes, in most cases. Losing job-based coverage — whether the job ended before or after open enrollment closed — typically triggers its own 60-day Special Enrollment Period, separate from the annual window. The timing of the job loss matters more than the timing of open enrollment.
Can I still get Medicaid or CHIP if I missed open enrollment?
Yes. Medicaid and CHIP don't follow the marketplace's annual open enrollment schedule. If your household income and circumstances qualify, you can apply for either program at any point during the year.
Does having a baby or getting married after the deadline reopen the window?
Generally yes. Marriage, the birth or adoption of a child, and similar family changes are standard qualifying life events that open a new 60-day enrollment window, regardless of when the annual open enrollment period ended.
What happens if I'm aging off a parent's plan and miss the window?
Turning 26 and losing eligibility for a parent's plan is itself a qualifying life event, which opens its own 60-day Special Enrollment Period. Missing the annual open enrollment window doesn't cancel that separate opportunity.
Are short-term health plans a real fallback if I missed open enrollment?
They can bridge a gap, but with real trade-offs. Short-term plans often exclude pre-existing conditions, don't have to cover the same essential benefits as marketplace plans, and generally don't count as qualifying coverage that avoids a future enrollment gap.
