Special Enrollment Period Explained
Your Health Insurance Options After a Life Event

Enrollment

Written by the InsurePlain Editorial Team · Reviewed for accuracy · Last updated August 2026

⚠️ Educational only. InsurePlain does not provide insurance, legal, or financial advice. Always consult a qualified insurance professional about your specific situation.
What's actually happening

You missed open enrollment. Or you're about to. Either way, you're staring down the idea of going months without health coverage, and that's a genuinely stressful place to be — one bad accident or diagnosis away from a bill that could wreck your finances.

Here's the part most people don't realize until they need it: Open Enrollment isn't the only time you can sign up. If something significant changed in your life recently — you lost a job, got married, had a baby, moved — you likely qualify for a Special Enrollment Period (SEP), a separate window that lets you enroll in or change coverage outside the normal calendar.

The catch is that SEPs are short and specific. You typically get 60 days from the date of the event, and if you let that window close without acting, you're usually back to waiting for the next annual Open Enrollment Period. Knowing whether you qualify — and moving fast if you do — is the whole game.

None of this requires guesswork on your part. The list of qualifying events is well defined, the documentation requirements are predictable, and the application itself takes most people under an hour once they have their paperwork ready. The hard part is simply knowing the clock is running and acting before it runs out.

Quick answers

What people ask most

What counts as a qualifying life event?
Losing coverage, marriage, a new baby, or a permanent move are the big four. Income changes and gaining citizenship status can also open a window, though the rules vary slightly by situation.
Can I get insurance without a qualifying life event?
Your options narrow, but you're not shut out entirely. Medicaid and CHIP accept applications year-round, and short-term plans or health sharing ministries can bridge a gap — just without ACA-level protections.
How long does a Special Enrollment Period last?
Usually 60 days from the qualifying event. Some Medicare-specific SEPs stretch longer — up to 8 months in certain employer-coverage situations — but the marketplace default is 60 days.
Qualifying life events

What actually triggers a Special Enrollment Period

Not every life change qualifies — voluntarily canceling a plan, for instance, doesn't. But a specific, recognized list of events does. If any of these happened to you in the last 60 days, start your SEP application now rather than researching further first.

  • Lost job-based or other health coverage — including layoffs, a plan being discontinued, or aging out of a parent's plan at 26. If you're weighing whether to bridge the gap with COBRA instead, it's worth comparing costs before you decide.
  • Got married — you and your spouse can enroll together, with coverage starting as early as the first of the following month.
  • Had a baby, adopted, or placed a child in foster care — coverage can be backdated to the day of the event, even if you enroll up to 60 days later.
  • Got divorced or legally separated and lost coverage — divorce alone doesn't qualify; you have to actually lose your health plan because of it.
  • Permanently moved — to a new ZIP code, county, or state where different marketplace plans are available.
  • Gained citizenship or lawful presence status — or had a change in income that affects your subsidy eligibility.
Your options compared

Qualifying event vs. no qualifying event

Whether you have a documented qualifying event changes what's actually available to you. Here's the honest split.

With a qualifying event

Full access to ACA marketplace plans, same subsidies as Open Enrollment, and the same protections — no medical underwriting, no denial for pre-existing conditions.

Without a qualifying event

Medicaid/CHIP is still open year-round. Otherwise you're looking at short-term health insurance or health sharing ministries — cheaper, but with real coverage gaps.

⚠️ The 60-day clock is strict

Most SEPs run 60 days from the event date, not 60 days from when you get around to applying. Mark the date the moment it happens, and don't wait to see if things "settle down" first — by the time they do, your window may already be closed.

How to enroll

Applying for your Special Enrollment Period

  1. Confirm your event qualifies. Check it against the list above, or call the marketplace call center if you're unsure.
  2. Gather your proof. A layoff letter, marriage certificate, birth certificate, or lease/utility bill showing your new address are common examples.
  3. Apply through HealthCare.gov or your state exchange. You'll report the event and upload documentation as part of the application.
  4. Compare plans quickly. If you also recently left a job, it's worth checking whether marketplace coverage or COBRA makes more financial sense for your situation.
  5. Pick a plan and pay your first premium. Coverage doesn't actually start until the first payment goes through.
  6. Save your confirmation. Keep the enrollment confirmation and effective date in case any coverage gap questions come up later.
What to do right now
If your qualifying event happened in the last 60 days, apply today — don't wait for the "right moment." If you've already missed the window and don't have a new qualifying event coming up, check whether Medicaid, a short-term plan, or COBRA can cover the gap until the next Open Enrollment Period.
Frequently asked questions
What is a Special Enrollment Period?
A Special Enrollment Period (SEP) is a window of time outside the annual Open Enrollment Period when you can sign up for or change health insurance. You qualify for one after certain life events, like losing coverage, getting married, or having a baby.
How long do I have to enroll during a Special Enrollment Period?
Most Special Enrollment Periods last 60 days from the date of your qualifying life event. Some Medicare-related SEPs run longer, up to 8 months in certain situations. Missing the window usually means waiting until the next Open Enrollment Period.
What counts as a qualifying life event?
Common qualifying life events include losing job-based or other health coverage, getting married or divorced, having or adopting a child, permanently moving to a new coverage area, and gaining citizenship or lawful presence status. Each event has its own documentation requirements.
Can I get health insurance if I don't have a qualifying life event?
Yes, but your options are more limited. Medicaid and CHIP enrollment is open year-round regardless of qualifying events. Outside of that, short-term health insurance and health sharing ministries can bridge a gap, though they don't offer the same protections as ACA-compliant plans.
What happens if I miss my Special Enrollment Period window?
If you miss your 60-day window, you'll typically need to wait until the next annual Open Enrollment Period to sign up for marketplace coverage, unless a new qualifying life event occurs in the meantime. Some limited options, like short-term plans, remain available in the gap.
