Home vs. Auto Insurance Deductibles
They Don't Work the Same Way

Home & Auto

Written by the InsurePlain Editorial Team · Reviewed for accuracy · Last updated August 2026

⚠️ Educational only. InsurePlain does not provide insurance, legal, or financial advice. Always consult a qualified insurance professional about your specific situation.
What's actually happening

You've probably heard the word "deductible" used the same way for your house and your car, so it's easy to assume they work identically. They don't.

A car deductible is nearly always a flat dollar amount — $500, $1,000, whatever you picked when you set up the policy. A home deductible can be that same flat amount, or a percentage of your home's insured value, depending on the type of damage — somewhat like how out-of-pocket maximums work on health plans.

That difference sounds small until a hailstorm hits your roof and your "deductible" turns out to be $4,000 instead of the $1,000 you pictured. Here's how each one actually works.

Quick answers

What people ask most

Is my home deductible the same number every time I file a claim?
Not necessarily. A standard deductible plus separate percentage deductibles for wind or hail changes the amount you owe based on the cause.
Can I have different deductibles for comprehensive and collision on my car?
Yes. Most policies let you split them — e.g. $250 comprehensive, $1,000 collision.
Does a higher deductible always mean lower insurance costs?
Generally, yes. More risk shifted to you usually means a lower premium, though savings vary by insurer.
The core difference

Flat dollar amount vs. percentage-based

Auto deductibles are fixed. If your collision deductible is $500 and the repair bill is $3,000, you pay $500 and the insurer covers the rest — that number never changes based on damage severity.

  • Standard home deductible — a fixed amount, typically $500–$2,000, for everyday claims like a burst pipe.
  • Percentage-based home deductible — 1–5% of your home's insured value, often for wind, hail, hurricane, or earthquake claims.
  • Auto comprehensive deductible — flat amount for non-collision damage like theft or storm damage.
  • Auto collision deductible — flat amount for accident damage, regardless of fault.
  • Liability deductible — usually none, on either a home or auto policy.
  • Per-claim vs. per-peril — home can carry more than one deductible type; auto keeps to one flat number per coverage type.
Side by side

How each deductible actually plays out

Applied to a real claim:

Home, percentage deductible

House insured for $300,000, 2% wind/hail deductible. Hailstorm causes $10,000 in roof damage. You owe $6,000, insurer pays $4,000.

Auto, flat deductible

Car has a $1,000 collision deductible. Accident causes $10,000 in damage. You owe $1,000 flat, insurer pays $9,000.

⚠️ Where people get caught off guard

Percentage deductibles are based on your home's insured value, not the repair cost. A high dwelling limit means a bigger out-of-pocket number than expected.

Before you file a claim

How to figure out what you actually owe

  1. Pull your declarations page. Both list every deductible near the coverage limits.
  2. Check for peril-specific deductibles. Look for separate lines for wind, hail, hurricane, or earthquake.
  3. Confirm flat vs. percentage. "2%" means math against your dwelling limit, not the repair estimate.
  4. Match the deductible to the coverage type. Auto policies split comprehensive and collision.
  5. Estimate the repair cost first. Close to your deductible? Filing may not beat a premium increase.
  6. Call your insurer if unclear. A short call beats a surprise bill.
What to do right now
Pull up your home and auto declarations pages and find the deductible section on each. If your home policy lists a percentage next to any peril, calculate the real dollar amount against your dwelling coverage limit now — before a damaged roof forces the math on you.
Frequently asked questions
Is a home insurance deductible the same as a car insurance deductible?
No. Home deductibles can be a flat dollar amount or a percentage of your home's insured value, while auto deductibles are almost always a flat dollar amount.
Why would my home insurance deductible be a percentage?
Insurers often use percentage deductibles for high-risk perils like wind, hail, hurricanes, or earthquakes — you might owe 1-5% of your home's insured value instead of a flat number.
Do I pay my auto deductible every time I file a claim?
Yes, for collision and comprehensive coverage. It applies per claim, not per year, so two separate claims could mean paying it twice.
Does liability coverage have a deductible?
No. Liability coverage generally doesn't carry a deductible because it pays for damage or injury you caused to someone else, not repairs to your own property.
Should I choose a higher or lower deductible?
A higher deductible usually lowers your premium but raises what you'd owe on a claim. The right choice depends on what you could comfortably pay out of pocket.
