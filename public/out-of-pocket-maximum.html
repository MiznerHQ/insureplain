Out-of-Pocket Maximum
What Actually Counts Toward It

Costs & Coverage

Written by the InsurePlain Editorial Team · Reviewed for accuracy · Last updated September 2026

⚠️ Educational only. InsurePlain does not provide insurance, legal, or financial advice. Always consult a qualified insurance professional about your specific situation.
What's actually happening

Your out-of-pocket maximum is the most misunderstood number on your entire insurance plan. Most people treat it like a simple ceiling — hit this number, stop paying — and that part is true. What trips people up is everything underneath it: which costs actually climb toward that ceiling, which ones don't count at all, and what "hitting the max" really changes once you get there.

This isn't the same thing as your deductible, and it doesn't work the way copays and coinsurance do on their own — it's the combined ceiling that sits above all of them. Confusing the three is one of the most common reasons people either panic over a medical bill unnecessarily, or get blindsided by one that shouldn't have surprised them.

The number itself usually shows up on your insurance card or in your plan documents without much explanation attached. Insurers rarely walk you through the fine print, and the fine print is where the real answers live — which is exactly why so many people either underestimate what a bad year could cost them, or overestimate how much protection that ceiling actually provides.

Below, we break down exactly what counts, what doesn't, how family plans complicate things, and what to actually expect once you hit your limit. If you're also trying to make sense of your broader coverage setup, our full insurance guides hub covers the related terms this one builds on.

Quick answers

What people ask most

Is my out-of-pocket maximum the same as my deductible?
No — your deductible is one piece of it. The out-of-pocket max is the total ceiling across your deductible, copays, and coinsurance combined. Your deductible is usually the smaller number you hit first.
What's a typical out-of-pocket maximum?
It varies widely by plan type and year. Marketplace plans have a federally set ceiling that adjusts annually, while employer plans can set their own limits below that ceiling. Check your Summary of Benefits and Coverage for your exact number.
Can my out-of-pocket maximum change mid-year?
Generally no, once your plan year has started. It's locked in when your coverage begins and holds steady until the next plan year, though switching plans or jobs can reset your progress toward it.
Does prescription drug spending count toward my out-of-pocket maximum?
Yes, for covered prescriptions. Copays and coinsurance you pay for medications on your plan's formulary count the same way medical copays and coinsurance do. Drugs your plan doesn't cover at all won't count, since there's no covered claim to apply toward the limit.
The confusing part

What counts toward your max

Only costs tied to covered, in-network medical care apply toward your out-of-pocket maximum. Here's what actually moves the needle, and what quietly doesn't:

  • Your deductible — every dollar you pay before coverage kicks in counts toward the max. See how this interacts with your plan in our deductible guide.
  • Copays — flat fees for office visits, urgent care, or prescriptions all count.
  • Coinsurance — your percentage share of a covered bill counts, right up until you hit the ceiling.
  • Premiums do not count — what you pay monthly to keep coverage active is separate and never applies.
  • Out-of-network care usually doesn't count — many plans exclude it entirely or track it against a separate, higher limit.
  • Non-covered services don't count — cosmetic procedures, services your plan excludes, or care denied for lack of medical necessity won't move you toward the max. If a claim gets denied, our claim denial guide walks through what to do next.
Family plans

How the embedded individual max works

Family plans add a layer most people never hear explained. Many plans use what's called an "embedded" individual limit sitting inside the larger family limit.

Embedded plan

If one family member's costs hit their individual max, their care is covered at 100% right away — even if the rest of the family hasn't spent enough to hit the family total yet.

Non-embedded plan

The whole family shares one combined max. No individual is "done" until the family total is reached, no matter how much one person alone has spent.

⚠️ CHECK YOUR PLAN DOCUMENTS

Not every plan uses embedded limits, and the terminology isn't always obvious on your insurance card. Call your insurer or check your Summary of Benefits and Coverage to confirm which structure your family plan uses.

Finding your number

How to track your progress toward the max

  1. Locate your Summary of Benefits and Coverage. This document lists your exact out-of-pocket maximum for individual and family coverage.
  2. Log into your insurer's member portal. Most portals show real-time progress toward both your deductible and your out-of-pocket max.
  3. Separate in-network from out-of-network spending. Only one of these usually counts, so don't assume every medical bill you've paid is credited.
  4. Check for embedded limits if you're on a family plan. Confirm whether individual members can hit their own ceiling early.
  5. Save your Explanation of Benefits statements. These show exactly how each payment was applied, which matters if your tracked total ever looks off.
  6. Note your plan year renewal date. Your progress resets to $0 on that date, along with your deductible.
What to do right now
Pull up your Summary of Benefits and Coverage and find your actual out-of-pocket maximum — individual and family, if applicable. Log into your insurer's portal to see how much you've already paid toward it this plan year. If you're close to hitting it, timing elective care, procedures, or specialist visits before your plan year resets can save real money, since anything you'd owe later in the year might cost you nothing once you cross the line. If your progress toward the max looks lower than you expected, double-check that every claim was processed as in-network — an out-of-network bill can quietly fail to count even when you assumed it would.
Frequently asked questions
Does my monthly premium count toward my out-of-pocket maximum?
No. Premiums are what you pay to keep your coverage active, and they never count toward your out-of-pocket maximum. Only costs tied to actual care — deductibles, copays, and coinsurance for covered services — count toward that number.
Once I hit my out-of-pocket maximum, do I really pay $0 for the rest of the year?
For covered, in-network services, yes — your plan is required to pay 100% of the allowed amount for the rest of the plan year once you hit the max. It does not apply to out-of-network care or non-covered services, and it resets when your plan year restarts.
How does the embedded individual max work inside a family plan?
Under an embedded family plan, each individual has their own out-of-pocket max built into the family total. If one family member hits their individual max, their costs are covered at 100% even if the family max hasn't been reached yet. Not every plan uses embedded limits, so it's worth confirming with your insurer.
Do out-of-network costs count toward my out-of-pocket maximum?
Usually not. Most plans only apply in-network costs toward your out-of-pocket maximum, and many don't cap out-of-network spending at all. Some plans track a separate, higher out-of-network maximum.
When does my out-of-pocket maximum reset?
It resets at the start of your plan year, which for most people is January 1st, though employer plans can use a different renewal date. It resets on the same schedule as your deductible, and both start over at $0 even if you hit your max the previous year.
